01 / Get your bearings

What do the forms actually do?

Start with one familiar verb. These examples use lese, “to read”.

INFINITIV / INFINITIVE

The dictionary form.

Jeg liker å lese.

I like to read. The infinitive often follows å. After a modal such as kan or skal, leave å out: Jeg kan lese.

PRESENS / PRESENT

Now, or as a habit.

Jeg leser hver kveld.

I read every evening. Many verbs add -r to the infinitive. The verb stays the same with jeg, du, hun, vi and the other pronouns.

PRETERITUM / PAST

A finished moment in the past.

Jeg leste i går.

I read yesterday. Learn this form with the verb: some follow a pattern, while others change much more, like gå → gikk.

PERFEKTUM PARTISIPP / PAST PARTICIPLE

A building block, not a tense on its own.

Jeg har lest boka.

I have read the book. Here, har plus lest makes the present perfect. The lookup shows the participle itself, without the helping verb.

One less thing to memorise. Norwegian verbs do not change for each person: jeg leser, du leser, vi leser. Learn the tense form once and use it with every subject.

03 / Put it in a sentence

The little words make a difference.

A plan for later.

Jeg skal lese i kveld.

I am going to read tonight. Skal + infinitive is useful for plans. It is one way to talk about the future, not a special ending added to the verb.

A simple instruction.

Les teksten!

Read the text! This is the imperative. With many verbs ending in -e, remove that final letter. Check the extra forms above for the dictionary’s entry.
