One has a few forms. Say en bil (one car), ei bok (one book) and ett hus (one house). The choice follows the noun’s gender. Accented én makes “one” especially clear. You can also use en bok in Bokmål.

02 / Put the pieces together

Small patterns. Bigger numbers.

TENS + ONES

Twenty + three = one word.

23 → tjuetre

Put the tens first, then the ones, with no space or “and”. The same pattern gives you førtiseks (46) and nittini (99).

HUNDREDS + OG

A little “and” goes a long way.

325 → tre hundre og tjuefem

Use og before a final part below one hundred: ett hundre og fem (105), or to tusen og ti (2 010).

03 / Numbers in real life

From the classroom to the café.

DATES & POSITIONS

First, second, third…

17. mai → syttende mai

An ordinal tells you the position, not the quantity. Add a dot after the digit: 1. is første, 2. is andre, 3. is tredje. Month names start with a lowercase letter.

YEARS

Two ways to think about a year.

1998 → nitten nittiåtte

Older years are often read in two parts. For 2026, to tusen og tjueseks works. Spaces here show the spoken parts; when spelling a year out in writing, join it: nittennittiåtte.

PRICES

A comma, not a decimal point.

49,50 kr

The full reading is førtini kroner og femti øre. In a shop you may simply hear førtini femti. Group thousands with spaces: 1 250,50 kr.

PHONE NUMBERS

Slow and clear is a good start.

12 → en · to

Read one digit at a time when sharing a number. Keep any zeros: null. Once you are comfortable, practise pairs, too: 12 can be read as tolv.
